# Whack Crash compliance audit, 2026-09-16

**Status:** research findings, not legal advice. Have a gaming lawyer and the test lab confirm everything before you submit.
**Scope:** commit `b684eaf` (branch `good-mole-and-whack-polish`), covering `apps/whack`, `packages/{core,fairness,engine,rgs-client}` and `apps/api`, against GLI-19 v3.0 and the markets in the skill's jurisdiction notes. Re-audit of `whack-crash-2026-09-15.md` after compliance-baseline groups 1–4 and 6.1, the `good-mole` change, and two decisions taken on 2026-09-16.
**Method:** code and spec read directly (every claim carries `file:line`); all committed RTP reports re-read and re-tabulated; pacing and presentation checked in the real client with an automated browser script, never by eye; four parallel research tracks re-checked the watch list against primary texts. Confidence: **H** primary text, **M** regulator page or several reliable secondary sources, **L** thin sources.

## 1. Verdict

The **server** is close to lab-ready: the platform work that the last audit called blocking (jurisdiction profiles, server-enforced cycle time, per-player lock, void/refund, disconnect policy, recall records with an operator API, platform crypto, seed rotation and encryption, append-only audit log, signed integrity manifest) is implemented and covered by tests. The **maths hold**: across 30 committed simulation reports no strategy beats the house, and the studio's cut is 3.00% ± 0.05 pp at normal stakes.

The **client is the blocker**. Four of the last audit's presentation gaps are still open — no rules screen, interactive decoy moles, the operator bridge still posting to `'*'`, and no clock, net position or reality check — and one new defect was introduced today: dropping the light profile's minimum cash-out to 0 made it possible to **celebrate a losing round**, which is a direct RTS 14F breach. None of this is deep work; it is compliance-baseline groups 5, 7 and 8, which are unstarted.

Not certifiable today. Nothing found requires a maths change.

## 3. What already passes

Verified in code and tests, not assumed:

- **Server-enforced minimum cycle time**, per market: 5 s UK/BR, 2.5 s ON/light (`packages/core/src/profiles.ts:112,129,143,157`), claimed atomically with the round lock (`packages/core/src/host.ts:367`, Redis Lua in `apps/api/src/redis-store.ts`). Verified in the real client with a browser script: after a round started at t=5 ms, a press at t≈1.08 s did not start a round. Automated, because the UKGC criticised stopwatch testing in the Stakelogic settlement (£122,835, 25 Jun 2026).
- **One active round per player**, across sessions and devices (`packages/core/src/host.ts:367`).
- **Void and refund on system failure**, idempotent, with an audit entry (`packages/core/src/round.ts:363`).
- **Disconnect policy per profile**, including cash-out-at-disconnect for the PT draft (`packages/core/src/host.ts:636`).
- **Recall**: every round records player, profile, config id, client version, balance before and after, return, net, result kind, crash multiplier, setbacks, boosts and cash-out evidence; retention defaults to 5 years (`apps/api/src/redis-store.ts:23`); operator JSON and CSV export behind an API key.
- **Platform crypto** (`node:crypto` on the server), **automatic seed rotation** (`packages/core/src/host.ts:716`), **encrypted seed storage** (AES-GCM, `apps/api/src/seed-cipher.ts:7`), **append-only hash-chained audit log** (`packages/core/src/audit.ts:65`), **signed build manifest with a self-check endpoint** (`apps/api/src/integrity.ts:54`).
- **No autoplay, no auto-rebet, one game at a time**; auto cash-out only ends the current bet.
- **The mock never ships**: the production build runs `scripts/check-no-mock.mjs`, and demo builds carry a visible DEMO badge.
- **Outcome secrecy**: a running round never exposes the crash time or future setbacks or boosts; verified by API tests that assert the serialised response contains neither.
- **Provable fairness**: commit before play, reveal on rotation, verifier reproduces crash time, setbacks and boosts.

## 4. The maths and the studio's cut

Re-tabulated from the committed reports (`packages/fairness/reports/`), not from the design.

**Theoretical (exact payouts, 10M rounds per id):**

| Config id               | Modifiers               | House cut, worst strategy | House cut, best strategy |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| `whack-crash/v1`        | setbacks x0.5           | 3.012%                    | 2.996%                   |
| `whack-crash/v1-rising` | none                    | 3.000%                    | 3.000%                   |
| `whack-crash/v2`        | setbacks + boosts x1.05 | 3.042%                    | 2.976%                   |
| `whack-crash/v2-rising` | boosts only             | 3.049%                    | 3.000%                   |

Every strategy is inside 97% ± 0.1%, and the largest deviations sit within about 2 standard errors of 3.000% — except one systematic effect: strategies that ride to the max win return ~0.04 pp less on boosted ids, exactly the cap-truncation cost predicted in `good-mole` design D12 (a boost can jump over the 10,000× cap and the excess is not paid). **No strategy beats the house.** The best measured player result is 97.024% ± 0.015 (auto cash-out x10 on `v2`), which is noise around 97%.

**With half-up rounding at the minimum stake (4M rounds, ±50 ms tap jitter):**

| Stake | `v1` house cut | `v2` house cut |
| ----- | -------------- | -------------- |
| 0.20  | 2.94% – 3.19%  | 2.99% – 3.14%  |
| 0.50  | 2.94% – 3.02%  | 2.99% – 3.08%  |
| 1.00  | 2.94% – 3.01%  | 2.99% – 3.08%  |

The worst single figure across all runs is `v1-rising`, "cash out at 2 s", stake 0.20: **96.682%**, i.e. the studio keeps 3.32%. The deviation is always in the studio's favour at small stakes, never the player's — so there is **no rounding path that lets a player beat 97%**, and no path where the studio quietly keeps more than the disclosed band (94.63%–99.37% at 0.20, in the report headers). The exposure is the reverse one: the advertised 97% is not met at the minimum bet level for some strategies, which is fix #7 above, not a maths defect.

**Instant bust** is 2.985%–3.009% across every report, against the 3.000% target (tolerance ±0.05 pp).

## 5. Findings on today's two reversals

- **`minCashout: 0` on the light profile** (cash-out allowed below x1.00). Legitimate in itself — a player being able to take what is left after a setback is player-favourable, and RTP is unchanged because a floor is a stopping time either way. But it makes a **losing "won" round** reachable, which turns fix #1 from theoretical into live. Regulated profiles keep x1.01 and run without setbacks, so their value never falls below x1.00. Brazil item 14(b)(iii) expects a **published minimum redemption multiplier**, so the BR profile must keep a non-zero floor and state it.
- **Removing the sound settings panel.** No rule requires per-channel volume control; mute remains reachable on the stage (`apps/whack/src/game/view.ts`). But the profile's `soundDefault` is still not honoured anywhere in the client, which matters for markets where sound must default to off — that is part of fix #5.

## 6. Watch-list results (re-checked today)

- **Brazil design portaria:** still **not published** in the DOU; the SPA legislation index ends at 13 Jul 2026. The 5 Aug 2026 briefing (5 s minimum, no autoplay/turbo, no loss celebrations, no skill suggestion, 30 days for design changes, 180 days for recertification) is explicitly preliminary (**H** for non-publication, **M/L** for the content).
- **Brazil item 14 — new and material:** the primary text defines a crash round ending only as manual redemption above the minimum, auto-redemption at the player's target, auto-redemption at the game maximum, or the multiplier **ceasing to increase** (14(d)(iv)), on a multiplier that "aumenta gradualmente". A **falling** multiplier fits none of those, so the x0.5 setback is a certification blocker in Brazil on primary text, not just by analogy with Portugal (**H**). Our `setbacksMode: 'off'` for `regulated-br` already handles this; the x1.05 boost is an increase and is not caught (**M**).
- **UK RTS 14G applies to crash games** (**H**): scope line "Casino (excluding slots and peer-to-peer poker)", 5 s minimum plus release-and-re-press. Our UK profile is 5 s and the guard is server-side. No extension of stake limits beyond reel-based slots was found.
- **GLI-19 v3.0 (Jul 2020) is still the current version** (**H**). No v4.
- **Rounding:** GLI-19 has no payout-rounding-direction rule (only §4.13.6(b) for jackpots and §4.3.5(c) for credit meters); Nevada Notice 2026-14 is cash-handling guidance and applies only by analogy; no MGA or UKGC rounding guidance exists. Half-up is neither mandated nor barred — **disclosure** is the live obligation (**H**).
- **Kenya — new hard blocker:** Conduct of Gambling Operations Regulations 2026 **reg 42(2)** requires player data to be stored and processed on **servers located within Kenya** absent written exemption, plus real-time GRA monitoring via API (reg 41(d)) and central-system integration (reg 44). US-hosted Vercel/Upstash fails this outright. Supplier licence exists (Licensing Regs 2026 reg 15, Form 12): KES 50,000 application, KES 500,000 a year, KES 5,000 per additional game; RNG and game rules certified annually with RTP disclosed (reg 45). **Reg 95(1)(d)** expressly bars "cartoons, toys, child-oriented images, or language likely to attract minors" — the Candy skin and the mole character are directly exposed (**H**).
- **Denmark:** a Danish **game supplier licence is already mandatory** (since 1 Jan 2025; 2026 fees DKK 67,600 application, DKK 45,100 a year, accredited certification before grant). Bill **L 127** (Spilpakke 1, tabled 25 Feb 2026) makes combination games need an **individual pre-launch approval per game** under § 18 stk. 5; passage not confirmed, provisions dated 1 Jul 2026 and 1 Jan 2027. Crash is not among the named game types, so Whack Crash would need an individual authorisation as a "variation" (**H** for the bill text, **M** for the inference).
- **Gibraltar:** the Gambling Act 2025 is **in force** (Act 2026-04, commenced 1 Apr 2026 by LN.2026/064). A crash supplier needs a **B2B Gambling Operator's licence** (s.17(1)(b), s.19(2)(b)/(h)) and must provide the service "in or from Gibraltar" (s.19(1)(e)), i.e. local substance. The Schedule 8 transitional cover lapses around **1 Oct 2026** (**H**); fee tiers sit in a separate Legal Notice that was not retrieved (**L**).
- **Curaçao:** foreign B2B suppliers need **registration only**, not a licence; the window is reported to open **October 2026** and the LOK transition ends **24 Dec 2026** (**M**, CGA notice reported; the registration dossier is not published).
- **Germany:** effectively closed — crash does not fit "virtuelle Automatenspiele" (defined as replicas of terrestrial slot machines) and no GGL statement on crash games exists. The €3/€5 stake pilot is slot-specific, runs to 31 Dec 2027 (**H**).
- **Nigeria:** the Central Gaming Bill 2025 was **denied assent** in Dec 2025, so the state-led regime stands. Lagos LSLGA publishes a B2B category but no fees, lab list or residency rules; the reported Global Lab exclusivity and July 2026 homologation window are **trade press only (L)**, and whether GLI certificates are accepted is **not found**.
- **Ghana:** no standalone supplier licence — a studio enters through a licensed operator, which files software certification at application and renewal. The modernising Legislative Instrument is still awaiting passage, and the Data Protection Bill 2025 is **not enacted**, so no residency rule applies today (**H**/**M**).
- **Colombia:** Coljuegos **Acuerdo 01 of 7 May 2026** exists and reportedly redefines instant-prize games around an RNG, a multiplier prize plan and player interaction deciding when to withdraw — which reads as opening the door to crash formats (**M**; the official PDF is an image scan, so the articles were not read). No falling-multiplier prohibition found.
