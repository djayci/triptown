# The Lift compliance audit, 17 Sep 2026

**Status:** research findings, not legal advice. A Nigerian and a Ghanaian gaming lawyer, plus your chosen test lab, must confirm everything before you submit or spend.

**Scope:** concept-stage audit of a crash game for **Nigeria and Ghana**, with the UK, Ontario, Brazil and Portugal checked for later expansion. The Lift is a **skin on the certified Whack Crash engine** — `registerGame('the-lift', 'whack-crash')` — so its maths, RNG and settlement are the platform's and are not re-derived here. One bet per round. The player rides a lift up an open shaft, a multiplier climbs, and the round ends **when the cable fails and the car drops**.

**The question this audit was run to answer:** whether a depicted mechanical accident can be the losing event. It can, and the premise that prompted the question was wrong.

**Method:** one research track against primary texts — CAP Code s.16 and s.4 in full, CAP/BCAP responsibility guidance, GCG Guidelines on Advertisement in full, Lagos gazette reg. 7 in full, ARCON Act and Vetting Guidelines, Portugal Reg. 308/2023 in full, Brazil Portaria 1.231/2024, the UKGC public register (downloaded 2026-09-17). Platform and shared-engine facts inherit from `whack-crash-2026-09-15.md` and the Nigeria/Ghana research in `night-meet-2026-09-16.md` and `going-viral-2026-09-16.md`.

**Confidence:** **H** primary text read, **M** regulator page or several reliable secondary sources, **L** thin sources.

---

## 1. Verdict

**The cable can fail. There is no rule against it in any market checked, and the category already does this.**

The concern that prompted this audit — that an ASA ruling had found a falling-jockey advert irresponsible — does not survive contact with the ruling. It is about free-bet mechanics and repeat play, and says nothing about the fall (§3 below). No rule, ruling or precedent against depicting a mechanical accident was found in any of the six markets.

Portugal, the only regulator on earth with written visual rules for crash games, **expressly permits** figurative animation (Reg. 308/2023 Rule 6). Destruction is the category norm: JetX's jet explodes, Space XY's rocket explodes, Zeppelin explodes, Balloon pops. And the lift *setting* already has UK-regulated precedent — Pragmatic Play's **Cash Elevator** ships from a UKGC licensee (account 56015, Active).

**One constraint does all the work: no person in the car, under it, or implied to be in it.** Portugal Rule 7(b) protects "a dignidade e integridade das pessoas"; Ghana's and Nigeria's violence clauses are person-scoped. That single line is what separates the JetX family, which is normal in regulated markets, from the Chicken Road family, where the character dies and which has **no verified regulated-market presence at all**.

The real risks for this game are the ones it already had: minors appeal from the arcade art direction, and the falling multiplier in Portugal. Neither is about the cable.

## 3. The premise that was wrong

This audit was commissioned because `night-meet-2026-09-16.md` records *"ASA Coral 2020: a falling-jockey video was irresponsible (M)"*, and a snapping cable looked like the same class of problem.

The full ruling was recovered. **ASA on LC International Limited t/a Coral, A20-1059159, upheld 3 June 2020** (**H**). The advert promoted a Cheltenham "Fail to Finish" free-bet offer; the fall is scene-setting in the description and **never reappears in the reasoning**. The complaint was that the ad "encouraged repeated gambling". The ASA's finding, verbatim, was that *"Have another go"* together with a man whose *"mood was instantly lifted following a free bet back"* gave the impression *"that the decision to gamble had been taken lightly"* and was likely to *"encourage some consumers to take up the offer repetitively"*. The breach was **CAP 16.3.1 only**.

**It is not authority for anything about accident or injury imagery.** The citation has been corrected in `night-meet-2026-09-16.md`.

## 4. What already passes

- **No CAP Code rule reaches peril.** Section 16 was read in full: no rule about danger, accident, injury, destruction or physical risk. The responsibility guidance PDF returns zero hits for danger, peril, accident, crash, injury or destruction (**H**).
- **CAP 4.2 draws its line at casualties and gratuitous shock, not hazard.** Upheld cases involve animated children bleeding, glass smashed over a head, gore, trivialised miscarriage. A fire-safety advert with graphic injury scenarios was **not** upheld (**H**). An empty car dropping sits far below all of it.
- **UKGC does not regulate game themes at all.** RTS 14 covers speed, autoplay, loss celebration and multi-game — nothing about imagery (**M**, absence).
- **Ghana's clauses do not engage.** Specific Guideline (viii) is a morality clause about "vices, general misconduct"; the Guidelines contain no reference to fear, distress, violence, shock, death, injury or accidents, and Act 721 has no advertising content standard at all (**H**).
- **Nigeria's reg. 7(1)(m) does not engage, and reg. 7 does not reach in-game art.** "Advertising" is defined in reg. 1 as *paid, mediated* persuasion; a game screen is neither (**H**).
- **Portugal permits it in writing.** Rule 5 allows a vertical trajectory; Rule 6 permits figurative video and audio animation whose meaning is unambiguous (**H**).
- **The lift setting has UKGC-licensed precedent.** Pragmatic Play's Cash Elevator and Gem Elevator; Pragmatic Play (Gibraltar) Ltd, UKGC account 56015, Active (**H**). A cable failure as the losing event is genuinely novel (**M**, absence).
- **Ontario and Brazil have no content rule of this kind** (**M**/**H**, absence).
- **The engine is unchanged.** The Lift resolves the same configuration as Whack Crash under every shipped profile, verified by test, so every committed RTP report covers it and nothing needs recertifying.

## 5. Recommended path

1. Keep the car empty, and keep it empty in the tile and the trailer too. That one rule is the audit.
2. Let the car leave the frame rather than land, and never ride it down.
3. Get Art. 54 of the Nigerian Code before any Nigerian launch.
4. Ship the adult skin for regulated markets; the arcade treatment is a bigger exposure than the cable ever was.
5. The falling multiplier is still the Portugal blocker (Rules 1/22) and is unrelated to this question.

### Defects found by this re-audit, and fixed

Three were found by running the game rather than reading it. All are fixed and re-verified.

1. **A lost round kept the live payout on screen.** `CrashScreen.showCrash` set the result card but
   never cleared the running figure, so a crash left the last animated amount in green above
   "THE CABLE WENT" — for example `₦107.84` on a round that lost `₦100.00`. That states winnings
   that do not exist (UK RTS 7E, AGCO 4.15) and reads as the amount the player would have collected,
   which is the near-miss framing banned by AGCO 2.15, GLI-19 §4.6.1(a) and RTS 7C. The settled
   paths now set the big value from the settlement and clear the live caption.
2. **The regulated profiles could not run at all.** `apps/lift/src/services.ts` omitted both
   `allowOverride` and `playerRegion`, so `ng-draft` threw at boot and every round was refused.
   No compliance check had ever reached a regulated profile for this game; the ones that appeared to
   pass were measuring the default profile (see below). Fixed, and both markets now run.
3. **The demo played under the engine's game id.** The client never called `registerGame`, so its
   rounds and recall records were filed as `whack-crash`. The API already registered `the-lift`
   correctly; only the demo client was wrong.

### A correction to how these checks were being run

`timing-check.mjs` and `presentation-check.mjs` parsed arguments loosely, and zsh does not
word-split an unquoted variable. A shell loop passing `$pf` where `pf="--profile ng-draft"` sent it
as one argv entry, shifting every later pair: the run used the default profile at the default
2500 ms gap and printed PASS under a regulated-market heading. Both scripts now reject a flag
containing a space or an unknown key, and `timing-check` fails with "the client never booted" rather
than reporting an infinite gap when the demo hook is missing. Measured again correctly, the Nigeria
and Ghana profiles enforce a worst gap of 5147 ms and 5149 ms against their 5000 ms minimum.

Two further gaps in the checks themselves were closed. `presentation-check` now reports that The
Lift emits no audio cues, so its celebration verdict rests on the confetti flag alone and the audio
half of each result is vacuous. And the even-return case is played at the market's minimum stake:
an at-stake return is a rounding outcome, and at a 10.00 stake the window is ±0.05% of x1.00, which
no cash-out round trip can hit — so that case had never once exercised the rule it exists for.

## 10. The ending was reframed, 17 Sep 2026 (supersedes fixes 1-3)

The cable no longer snaps. The lift **arrives** at the floor it was always going to, and the doors
open. This was a product decision — the fall made a poor round and a worse story — but it changes
the compliance position materially, so section 2's first three fixes are restated here rather than
left standing as written.

**Fixes 1, 2 and 3 were all consequences of the fall, not independent rules.**

- Fix 1 (nobody in the car) rested on Portugal Reg. 308/2023 Rule 7(b), dignity and integrity of
  persons, and Ghana AAG Art. 15, violence against people. Both require something bad to happen to
  someone. A lift that stops at a floor harms nobody, so the rule no longer reaches this game.
  **A rider now stands in the car** — an adult in a coat with a case, small head on broad shoulders,
  which is the proportion that reads as an adult rather than a child (CAP under-18 guidance Oct 2025
  §14, CAP 16.3.14, PT R7c, Kenya reg 95(1)(d)).
- Fix 2 (no impact, no falling point of view) is moot: nothing falls, so there is nothing to land and
  no camera to ride down. The CAP 4.2 fear-or-distress exposure that motivated it is gone entirely
  rather than mitigated.
- Fix 3 (no dread build-up) **still stands, and is now the load-bearing one.** It no longer protects
  against a fear effect; it protects the no-advance-warning rule. A lift that slowed, chimed or
  flickered as it neared its floor would be telling the player the round was about to end. The
  arrival is therefore abrupt by design: `onCrash()` sets a flag and the doors open *after* the
  outcome, so the only animation the stop produces is one the player already knows the result of.

**What is unchanged.** Every animated property is still a function of the multiplier alone. The new
zones (Lobby, Offices, Sky Lobby, The Terrace, Penthouse, The Roof) are entered at multipliers, not
times, so two rounds reaching the same value have passed through the same building — pinned by a
test, alongside the existing one for speed. The zone names live in the message catalogue, so they
are translatable and reviewable with the rest of the copy (GLI-19 §4.4.1, PT R7), which
`copy-check.mjs` caught when they were first hardcoded in the scene.

**A copy defect this found.** The Lift's rules text described setbacks and boosts as "a bad mole"
and "a good mole" — Whack Crash's wording, inherited when the catalogue was copied. A player reading
the rules of a lift game was being told about moles. Rewritten as drops and express jumps, and a new
`rules.decoration` entry states that the floor and the zone are decoration over the multiplier, that
where the lift stops is fixed when the round starts, that it cannot be predicted, and that tapping
changes nothing (GLI-19 §4.4.1, AGCO 2.15, RTS 7C).

**Still open, unchanged:** fix 4 (marketing copy framing), fix 5 (Nigerian Code Art. 54, unread) and
fix 6 (ARCON and GCG pre-approval of assets that do not exist yet). Nothing here is legal advice.

## 11. Open blocker found after this audit: the information surfaces are unreachable

Recorded the same day, and **this audit missed it.** Sections 9 and 10 verified the concept-stage
fixes and the presentation and timing behaviour, and never checked that a player could reach the
rules at all. It surfaced when another session's user noticed there was no sound in `apps/gate`.

`CrashScreen` wires exactly one handler, `onBigButton`, and `setMuted()` has an empty body. The
Lift's `main.ts` constructs `RulesPanel`, `HistoryPanel` and `FairnessPanel` and passes
`onRules` / `onHistory` / `onFairness` to the controller, so all three panels are built on every
boot and **none of them can be opened**. There is also no sound control, no stake +/- and no auto
toggle. This affects every game built on `CrashScreen`, which is The Lift and Beat the Gate.

| Surface | Required by | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Rules and help before any bet | GLI-19 §4.4.1, UK RTS 3/4, compliance rule 3 | **FAIL** — panel exists, no control opens it |
| Per-round history and recall | compliance rule 7 | **FAIL** — same |
| Fairness / seed verification | compliance rule 13, and this game's own rules text | **FAIL** — same |
| Sound control | not a blocker, but `soundDefault: 'muted'` on both African profiles means silence with no way back | **FAIL** |

The sharpest version is a disclosure defect rather than a missing control. `rules.provablyFair`
reads "Every round can be verified from its seeds after the seed is revealed. **Open the fairness
panel to check.**" The published rules instruct the player to do something the client makes
impossible, so the statement is false as shipped. The same string is in Beat the Gate's catalogue.

`triptown-games-bc` is adding the controls to `CrashScreen`. Four constraints agreed with them:
rules must be reachable **before any bet**, not only mid-round; the controls must stay enabled
through the minimum-cycle countdown, which is when a player is most likely to read them; no control
may sit inside the big button's `RESULT_INPUT_GUARD_MS` input guard, or its taps would be swallowed
for 700 ms after a result; and each needs an accessibility label, since the HUD is Pixi and the DOM
layer is all a screen reader sees.

**Why the audit missed it, for the next one:** every check it ran drove the game through the single
control that does work. A check that plays rounds cannot see a surface that no round touches. The
presentation and timing checks should not be read as evidence about anything a player reaches by any
other route. To be verified and this section closed once the controls land.

## 12. Section 11 closed, and a second thing the checks could not see

**The controls landed and were verified by opening them, not by reading the code.** Driving the
built client under `ng-draft` before placing any bet, each icon was clicked and the resulting panel
read back from the DOM:

| Control | Result before any bet |
|---|---|
| Rules ("?") | opens "How this game works" — **PASS**, GLI-19 §4.4.1, RTS 3/4 |
| Fairness (shield) | opens the provably-fair panel — **PASS**, and the `rules.provablyFair` text is now true |
| History (clock) | opens "Round history" — **PASS**, compliance rule 7 |
| Sound (speaker) | nothing happens — see below |

Evidence: `lift-controls-ng-draft.png`. Checks re-run clean afterwards on all three profiles:
presentation PASS on `light`, `ng-draft`, `gh-draft`; timing worst gaps 2588 ms (2500 min),
5168 ms and 5172 ms (5000 min); copy PASS.

**A contrast defect the screenshot showed and no check did.** On the adult skin the multiplier was
drawn in `sun` (0x2c333b, a dark charcoal) on this game's stage, which is a flat fill of `ink`
(0x14161a). That is **1.42:1** — the primary value, effectively invisible, against a 3:1 floor
(BR Annex I item 14(c), PT regra 4, AGCO 4.15). The 88px stroke does not rescue it, because the
stroke is `ink` too and so the same colour as the ground. The payout's `lime` was only 3.08:1.

The check that exists for exactly this ran only when a game *recoloured*, on the stated assumption
that "the shipped palettes are reviewed against the stages they were drawn for". That assumption is
true only of the stage each palette was drawn for. The Lift was not one of them. The gate is now
whether a game declares its `ground` at all, and The Lift declares it and takes brass (7.83:1) and a
lighter teal (6.87:1) on the adult skin — muted, and legible.

**The sound control is dead in this game.** The Lift passes `null` for audio, and the controller's
`onSound` returns early when there is none, so the button is present, tappable, and does nothing:
the icon does not even change. It is not a decoy in the AGCO 2.15 sense — it is a real control wired
to a real handler — but it tells the player sound exists when it does not. Raised with the session
that owns `CrashScreen`; the fix is for the controller to tell the view whether audio exists so the
control can be hidden. **OPEN.**

**The pattern, twice in one day.** Both of these were invisible to every automated check and obvious
in a screenshot: a surface no round touches, and a colour no assertion covered. The checks answer
"does playing a round behave correctly". They do not answer "can a player reach this" or "can a
player see this", and this audit should not be read as evidence about either beyond what is recorded
here.
